Variant Explorer in Views
Introducing the Variant Explorer

The Variant Explorer (also “VE” for short) is a View component that allows you to discover all of the different ways in which the business processes flow within your organization. Each of these unique ways is called a “variant”. In other words, a variant represents objects that follow the same path and sequence of events within your process.

Reviewing the Variants allows you to understand whether the ways in which processes run in your organization follow your desired flows.

With the Variant Explorer, you can:

Review variants individually.

Compare multiple variants to one another.

Review the variants with process metrics or the Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) available in your dataset.

Use the various filtering options.

The Most Common Variant

As mentioned before, the Variant Explorer displays the most common variant by default.

Let's get started by reviewing the most common variant in this dataset, which means the largest group of objects going through the same set of events, following the same sequence. The number of objects going through each connection is the same throughout the process, as you're viewing only one variant at this time.

What is the percentage of objects covered by this variant?

Answer

When you review the events from start to end, you'll see that most often in this process, (1) sales order headers are created, (2) sales order items are created,  and then, (3) the sales order is approved.

In this VE, under each event header you see the amount of times each event has occurred. Please note that, depending on how your Data Analyst configured your View, you might see other relevant KPIs in this area as well.

Switch the Process Metric

So far, so good! But the object counts tell us only so much about what is going on in our business processes. It’s time to switch the process metric that is being displayed. 

Go over to your View, and switch to the Throughput Time (Median) metric. Take a look at the new numbers being displayed, and then, come back to the course to answer the question below.

How much longer does it take when the sales order goes through the "Set Billing Block" and "Release Billing Block" events (compared to the sales orders where the process ends right after the "Approve Sales Order" event)?

Answer
--- Page 11 ---
Use the Variant Explorer in Views
Variant Explorer in Views
Filter on Objects Using Events

To dive deeper into the data and reveal further process insights, let’s focus on the "Set Billing Block" event.

Click on this event to reveal more information about it, and then filter your dataset to show you only the objects that go through this event.

After applying the filter, pay attention to the upper right corner of the View. It informs you that you have filtered your data to only show you the subset which includes only the sales orders that go through the  Set Billing Block" event.

You can always edit this filter to make adjustments. You can also remove it to go back to viewing the entire dataset, or opt for disabling the filter so that you can easily enable it again if you wish to come back to it.

Note that once you add a filter, the entire View updates to show you the data that corresponds to the subset you have filtered for. For example, check out the new numbers on the KPI list.

What’s the Net Order Value of the sales orders that go through the  Set Billing Block" event?

Answer

When you want to filter for a certain event, you can always use the built-in event filter from the top of the View, instead of interacting with the Variant Explorer. You can also leverage the various options here to customize your filter.

Remember to remove or disable any filters you have applied before moving on!
--- Page 12 ---
Use the Variant Explorer in Views
Variant Explorer in Views
Filter on Objects Using Connections

You can also filter your dataset by using connections.

To do so, simply click on the connection that you’re interested in, just like how you filtered on the objects using an event.

Time to try it for yourself! Start by removing any filters and superimpose variants #1 and #3 again. And then, filter for the sales orders that go from the "Approve Sales Order" event to the "Set Billing Block" event.

Note that once you apply a filter, the entire View updates to show you the data that corresponds to the subset you have filtered for. This time, let's check out the updated variant list.

What is the median throughput time for the most common variant of the dataset you filtered for (i.e., the sales orders that go from the Approve Sales Order directly to the Set Billing Block event)?

Answer

When you want to filter for a certain connection, you can always use the built-in process flow filter from the top of the View, instead of interacting with the Variant Explorer. You can also leverage the various options here to customize your filter.

Remember to remove or disable any filters you have applied before moving on!

Check out Celonis Docs for in-depth information on all filter types. We will be releasing even more filtering options, so stay tuned!

Introducing the Variant Explorer

The Variant Explorer (also “VE” for short) is a View component that allows you to discover all of the different ways in which the business processes flow within your organization. Each of these unique ways is called a “variant”. In other words, a variant represents objects that follow the same path and sequence of events within your process.

Reviewing the Variants allows you to understand whether the ways in which processes run in your organization follow your desired flows.

With the Variant Explorer, you can:

Review variants individually.

Compare multiple variants to one another.

Review the variants with process metrics or the Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) available in your dataset.

The Variant Explorer (also “VE” for short) is a View component that allows you to discover all of the different ways in which the business processes flow within your organization. Each of these unique ways is called a “variant”. In other words, a variant represents objects that follow the same path and sequence of events within your process.

Reviewing the Variants allows you to understand whether the ways in which processes run in your organization follow your desired flows.

The Variant Explorer (also “VE” for short) is a View component that allows you to discover all of the different ways in which the business processes flow within your organization.
